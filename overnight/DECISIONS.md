# Decisions made overnight (no one awake to ask)

Format: what, why, alternatives.

1. **Rapier 0.14 (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) as the rigid-body engine.** The spec asks for it; 0.14.0 is a stable release well over two weeks old. Wrapped in `src/sim/phys/`, so gameplay code never touches Rapier directly.
2. **FUEL compliance = rigid contacts + speed-dependent restitution, not a per-contact spring.** Rapier's contact softness is global (it's for penetration recovery, not material compliance). The whitepaper puts FUEL at ≈20 lbf/in (≈3.5 kN/m) → a 0.215 kg ball has a ~25 ms contact, which a 240 Hz rigid solver can't resolve with a spring anyway. So: e(v) curve per surface (fit to "rebounds well under half its drop height", livelier on hard panels, dead on the net) + a visual squash lasting the real contact time.
3. **Field collision comes from the real field model** (AdvantageScope 2026 field, BSD-3, already in the repo), extracted offline into a collision mesh per surface type, not hand-made boxes. The carpet is a flat ground plane.
4. **Robot-frame hopper physics is replaced by real bodies inside the robot's colliders**, so the hopper jostles from the real chassis motion (no pseudo-forces).
5. **High Score mode: the Hub is always active** (spec section 3.6). Balls in flight at 0:00 count if they score within 3 s — the manual's own rule (6.5: "assessment of FUEL scored in the HUB continues for up to 3 seconds after the ARENA timer displays 0:00").
6. **Our alliance is always internal −x.** Playing blue turns the whole rendered scene 180° and swaps the alliance colors (the field is rotationally symmetric). Physics, brains and tests never need a mirrored copy.
7. **Physics runs in a Web Worker at 120 Hz.** Rapier costs ~5 µs per awake ball per step; with the robot plowing the neutral-zone pile (~300 awake) that is 2–3 ms per step, which would blow the 4 ms per-frame budget on the main thread. In the worker the render thread spends ~1 ms per frame on the simulation. 240 Hz doubled the cost with no visible difference. Tests can still step it on the main thread (`?physics=main`).
8. **Thin field parts are solid boxes.** The TRENCH arms and TOWER uprights/rungs are 4–16 cm thick; as triangle shells the robot could pass through them (the Tower bug Jack saw). They're now solid steel colliders from the manual's dimensions, placed where the field model has them.
9. **Wheelies are allowed.** A stowed intake hitting a TRENCH arm 0.6 m up at full speed pops a brief wheelie (the impact is far above the center of mass). Realistic friction makes it fall back; the driver can reverse out.
10. **The autopilot crosses BUMPs with momentum (≤ 3.4 m/s).** The CAD puts the belly 4.8 cm up; the BUMP peak is 0.16 m with 15° ramps, so a robot that crawls over beaches itself. Ask the team whether the real belly clearance is higher.
11. **Intake button = on/off.** On deploys the intake and runs the rollers; off stops and stows it. (A stowed robot is 0.728 m tall: it no longer fits under a TRENCH, which is what the CAD says.) Keyboard R still raises/lowers without the rollers.
12. **Free Drive keeps the self-driving robot** (left alone, it plays Endless; touch anything and it's yours; 15 s later it drives itself again). Auto mode and its scripted routine were removed from the UI to keep it minimal.
13. **Personal best is stored on the main thread** (the worker has no localStorage).
14. **Sound is physically driven and off by default.** Drive gear whine follows motor speed and current, flywheel hum follows actual RPM (the dip of each shot is audible), foam impacts are low thuds, rate-limited. I could not listen to it tonight, so it ships off; Jack should judge it (Settings → Robot Sound).
15. **Flywheel inertia 0.004 kg·m²** (EDIT). No 2026-specific data was found; heavier wheels are the common advice so a shot costs ~3 % speed.
16. **Ferry landing spot**: x = −6.3 m (our zone, clear of the HUB and TOWER), z = robot z × 0.8, clamped to ±2.6 m.
17. **Net = hysteretic spring-damper** (stiff stretching, 8 % stiffness springing back), not a cloth simulation: it stops a 20 m/s ball within a few cm of the plane and drops it at 0.4 m/s.
18. **TOWER colliders were mirrored in z (a real bug), now placed on the field model's uprights.** `addTowers` put our (−x) tower at z = −0.295 while the model's uprights are at z = −0.140 and +0.717 (center +0.289; the +x tower is its 180° rotation, center −0.289). The mesh's own uprights were culled by `build-collision.mjs` (correctly, for the solid boxes), so the robot drove through the drawn uprights and stopped at invisible ones. `tests/phys/collision.test.ts` measures the uprights from the .glb and drives into them.
19. **HUB net frame: slanted struts from the HUB top (y 1.27) to the top bar, not floor-to-ceiling posts.** The posts at z = ±0.725 stood on the carpet (x −3.055…−2.775 for our HUB) where the model has nothing below 1.27 m, so a robot passing the HUB's neutral side hit invisible 3 cm posts. A robot (≤ 0.728 m) now passes under the frame; FUEL still hits it.
20. **Planner grid (`field.ts`): HUB block is ±0.6 m around its center (was the full bounding box incl. the net frame, ±0.742 × to x = −2.77), and the TOWERs are solid** (the grid's rays start at 0.45 m and missed their uprights and rungs entirely, so the autopilot could plan straight into them).
21. **Team code port (`phys/teamcode.ts`, 50 Hz, WPILib frame).** The Java (Rebuilt26 `insert-name-here`) runs as written: DriveCommands shaping/discretize/desaturate/optimize/cosineScale, ProfiledPID face-HUB, GetAdjustedShot lookahead and table (interpolate inside, extrapolate outside), Turret.selectSafeSetpointDegrees, DefaultModeBindings (LT/RT/RB/B/Start+B/Y/A/D-pad, neutral-zone ferry). The AI uses the same layer: its path follower is turned into stick positions by inverting the team's shaping.
22. **Heading estimate**: Y sets the estimate's heading to 0; the error decays with τ = 2 s (the Limelights pull it back). Position estimate = truth. The robot starts with the intake stowed and the turrets at −180°, like power-up.
23. **Turret motor ratio 20:1 (config `TURRET.motorGear`)**, not 10: with the team's gains a 10:1 X44 turret overshoots a 360° unwind by 100° through its soft limit; 20:1 is the lowest ratio that does not. Motors: Kraken X44 (azimuth, flywheels), X60 (drive, steer, intake, indexer); rotor inertias are estimates. The 120 Hz hardware loop uses implicit solves (back-EMF, D terms, voltage ramp) and 4 sub-steps for azimuth/steer/pivot.
24. **Hood/exit fit**: elevation = 82° − hood, exit speed 0.91·(wheel surface speed)/2, backspin v/R (rms 0.14 m on the table). Known limit: 2 m is the closest HUB shot that clears the rim in the sim (1.5 m shots hit the HUB front); the closest row in the team's table is 1.3 m.
25. **Intake arm is a DOF**: pivot motor (Motion Magic, kG as written: +1 V toward deployed) vs gravity and the normal impulses on the intake hull; the hull stays rigidly attached to the chassis for the solver (momentum absorbed by the arm is not subtracted: documented approximation). Friction impulses and inertial pseudo-forces on the arm are ignored.
26. **Indexer/hotdog friction is calibrated from the team's kV** (kP = 0, so kV sets the loaded speed); lane belt/floor speeds tuned to 4 balls/s per turret with a full hopper. A captured ball between the rollers drops out 0.4 s after LT is released.
27. **Autopilot** holds LT only near its target ball (LT caps speed at 2.5 m/s), shoots with RT in our zone, re-triggers LT (release, press) to redeploy after a stow; scores at load ≥ 24. 60 s, seed 3: 78 scored (was 110 with the old free-speed robot).
28. **Crawling over a BUMP beaches the robot on its belly; driving over it doesn't (kept, like the real robot).** The BUMP is a 0.165 m peak with ~16° faces. With the modules 0.55 m apart and the CAD's lowest belly parts 3.5–4.8 cm up, a level robot straddling the peak has its wheels on the faces about 8 cm up, so the belly sits on the peak with the wheels unloaded. Measured (driving straight across, both BUMPs beside our HUB, intake up and down): full stick crosses; stick 0.6 (1.4 m/s) stops on the peak; started on the peak it can't move. A real robot built this low would do the same. EDIT: Jack, if the real robot crawls over bumps, its belly clearance is higher than the CAD's; give me the number.
29. **Driver controls follow the team's RobotContainer, with raw sticks.** The renderer sends the stick unshaped (clamped to length 1) because the robot code applies the team's deadband and squaring; only "is someone driving" uses a 0.12 drift threshold so a loose stick doesn't take the robot over from the AI. Triggers count as held past 0.5 (`leftTrigger(0.5)`). B alone = unjam, Menu + B = reverse intake only (the chord is fixed; unjam's button is rebindable). The bindings key is `chuck-norris-controls-v3`, so everyone gets the new defaults.
30. **Touch Intake is a latch standing in for "hold LT".** Tap on: deploy and run the rollers; tap off: the rollers stop and the intake stays down, exactly like releasing the trigger. A separate Up button is RB. Up (or any retract) also clears the latch, otherwise the held intake would immediately deploy it again.
31. **Tab = next camera.** It is only taken while the sim is running and no sheet (settings, Controls page) is open, so keyboard focus still moves through the sheets and form fields. Shift (either) is the keyboard intake, so it no longer means "precision" (the team code has none).
32. **The scoreboard replaces the score pill.** One top-center glass card level with the exit and settings buttons: FUEL scored in big numerals, the High Score clock to its left only in High Score, a FUEL/s cell only while shooting. The "Robot driving" note, ferry and range chips and the first-time hint sit below it.
33. **The CAD Move button is removed with its tray.** Mechanisms are still driven by press-and-hold dragging, the subsystem inspector's sliders, the tour and the sim; only the separate tray of sliders is gone.
34. **Wiring is procedural (`src/wiring.ts`), built at load into two merged meshes (insulation, braided sleeving) inside the electrical subsystem.** In the CAD the "brain pan" mechanism is only the polycarbonate door; the electronics hang under the aluminum plate and stay put when it opens, so the harness is fixed to the plate, not the door. Layout follows the CAD device positions (PDH, MPM, roboRIO, Pigeon, CANivore are already in the CAD as unnamed "Part"s) and the photos (red/black leads from the PDH levers, yellow/green twisted CAN with WAGO splices, grommets at walls and hinge rail, zip ties). Hidden in the simulation (underside rarely seen). Not wired: turret flywheel and intake motors (they move with mechanisms), Limelights, radio, RSL, USB/ethernet; riser sleeving goes only to the two turret rotation motors. All wires carry the PDH part id, so they ghost/hide/pick as the electrical subsystem.
35. **The cargo net is an XPBD diamond-mesh cloth in the world frame, stepped before the engine, not in the robot frame.** The pins carry the robot's motion, so gravity and the robot's accelerations sway it with no pseudo-forces; sweeping each ball through the cloth's 6 substeps before Rapier moves it means nothing tunnels. Alternatives: robot-frame cloth with inertial forces (needs angular terms for every spin), implicit springs (a 3 g knot against a 215 g ball needs a global solve each step). Knots are the diamond mesh itself (cords at ±45° to the edges, 5.2 cm), not a square grid drawn rotated, because only the cords carry load.
36. **Net damping is high on purpose.** A lightly damped cloth is a trampoline (a 1 m drop would bounce nearly as high as off the carpet); the real net is dead from knot friction. Cord damping 70 s⁻¹ gives a 14 cm rebound (carpet 24 cm). EDIT: drop a FUEL on the real net.
37. **Net sag is designed, then checked**: 0.33 % cord slack at the deployed length gives 4.7 cm at the middle (photos: "a few cm"). Stowed, the front edge moves back 0.24 m and up 0.18 m, so the cloth hangs in a bag, kept above the hopper floor (0.31 m) and between the walls; the real net presumably does something similar.
38. **The net hands the robot the reaction of what it holds.** A ball resting on the net loads the pins, so the robot feels it (a few newtons per ball). The cloth's own inertial reaction on the robot (0.5 kg) is ignored.
39. **Panels are one lumped oscillator pair per panel with the stiffness of a 20 cm clamped bay and the mass of the whole panel.** Plate theory for the whole 0.48 × 0.44 m wall gives ~30 Hz and a 5 cm deflection under a 10 m/s ball, which is not what a bolted, braced, perforated robot panel does (spec: a few mm). The frame's support pitch is an estimate (EDIT: measure it); with it, 10 m/s gives 4 mm, a wall hit with the intake out gives ~2 mm, and the numbers move together with `PLATE.bay`.
40. **A panel's ball strike is integrated through the contact (foam spring + modes), then the ball's rebound is only ever reduced.** Rapier's rigid bounce stays the baseline (so every existing test still holds); the panel takes its energy out of the rebound. The CCD does not stop a 10 m/s ball at a 2.4 cm wall (it sinks ~7 cm before being pushed out); strike detection accepts that.
41. **Frame hits excite the panels as a 4 ms half-sine pulse.** The solver resolves a wall hit in one 8.3 ms step; real bumper foam takes ~4 ms, and a 170 Hz panel (6 ms period) sees that as a pulse, not a step. EDIT: measure the pulse with an accelerometer.
42. **The net draws as crossed ribbons, not a texture.** Cords are 2.2 mm wide and 5 cm apart; any alpha-tested texture would shimmer at distance and could not follow the cloth's real deformation per cord. 2.6 k vertices per frame, written on the CPU.
43. **The CAD view shows the same cloth at rest and re-settles it when the intake moves** (≤ 20 Hz, ~3 ms), parented to the hopper subsystem so explode and focus carry it. It hides while the simulation draws its own.

## Sim visuals, POVs and performance (Sonnet, visuals task)
- **Weird chrome block on the left**: not a motor. `chuck-norris-sim.glb` has no hardware, so each mesh's meshopt quantization bounds (the scale/offset on its node) differ from the full-detail mesh's; `setSimRendering` swaps the bare geometry under the full-detail node, so the frame's steel `Part` bracket (2×6×7 cm) drew 1.7× larger as a chrome block beside the intake. Fixed in `Simulation.rebaseLod` (index.ts): every LOD vertex goes through the LOD node then the inverse full-detail node. Same fix improves every other mesh by a fraction of a millimetre. Proved by hiding the part (pid 1353) before/after: `scratchpad/visuals/before|after/left-mid.png`.
- **Hood = the toothed plates + shell + two side rails**, pivoting on the flywheel's hex shaft (the toothed plate's tooth arc is centered on it, radius 0.203 m pitch). The servo pinion (10T, 10 DP) turns −16× the hood's angle. Baked as hidden mechanisms nested inside the turret's node (`parent` in subsystems.json; `hood-*`, `hoodgear-*`, `flywheel-*`), so they yaw with the turret and exist in both GLBs. In the CAD viewer they are hidden (no slider) but long-pressing a hood part drags the hood.
- **Hood zero**: the CAD pose is hood ≈ 17.2° (shell end 25.2° above the shaft axis → exit tangent 64.8° above horizontal = 82° − 17.2°). EDIT in hood.ts: confirm with the real hood at a known servo pulse. A higher hood angle rotates the shell up, i.e. a flatter shot, as the config says.
- **Turret yaw**: `Frame.turrets[].a` is unwrapped (−380°…10°); the mechanism clamp stopped the mesh at ±180°. Now wrapped (the rotation is periodic).
- **Flywheel spin** is the real speed / 16 (a real 300+ rad/s strobes at 60 fps); direction and rate follow `fly`.
- **POVs**: robot (above the intake roller, outside the cage, near plane 3 cm), turret (behind/over the left turret along its aim, slew rate capped), broadcast (sideline rail camera, lagged), chase low (bumper height, wall-aware distance). Robot and turret cameras slide back inside the field walls; turret camera climbs over the TRENCH/HUB like Follow. View changes ease over 0.6 s. The list lives in `src/sim/views.ts` (no three.js) so Settings and the sim share it.
- **Performance**: measured in software GL (absolute fps meaningless, compare numbers). Physics is 2.3 of 2.8 ms/step in Rapier itself (avg; p99 22 ms on contact bursts); left alone because any solver/sleep change alters behavior. Render side: the hologram "ghost" copies of the CAD viewer were visible for the first ~0.3 s of every sim run (850k extra triangles): hidden at start; FUEL draws only balls on the field (instance count) with a 16×10 sphere (was 20×14); per-frame allocations in the fuel loop removed.
